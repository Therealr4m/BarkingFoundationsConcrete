Can You Build Foundations on Clay Soil?

Yes, you can build foundations on clay soil. In fact, clay is a very common ground condition for residential foundation work around Barking and across East London. The important point is that clay cannot simply be treated the same as firm, stable ground.

We regularly come across situations where homeowners have been told that clay makes a foundation impossible, when that is not really the case. What clay does mean is that the foundation needs to be designed and installed with ground movement in mind.

After years of carrying out groundwork, the biggest problems we see are rarely caused by the fact that the soil is clay itself. They usually come from poor excavation, unsuitable founding depth, inadequate preparation or failing to account for nearby trees and changing moisture levels.

Why Is Clay Soil Different?

Clay behaves differently from many other soils because its volume can change as its moisture content changes. During prolonged wet conditions, clay can absorb water and expand. During prolonged dry periods, it can lose moisture and shrink.

That movement matters when a building or extension is sitting above it. If a foundation is too shallow or is founded in unsuitable material, seasonal ground movement can contribute to cracking, settlement or movement in the structure above.

This is why we do not look at a patch of ground and simply decide on a foundation depth from appearance. The actual ground condition, proposed structure, nearby vegetation, loading and site circumstances all need to be considered.

How Deep Do Foundations Need to Be in Clay?

There is no single depth that is correct for every clay site. This is one of the most important things for homeowners to understand before starting foundation work.

The required depth depends partly on the type of clay and how much it is expected to move. Foundation depth can also need to increase where trees or substantial vegetation are close to the proposed structure because roots can influence moisture levels within the surrounding soil.

UK building guidance recognises this issue. Approved Document A includes specific provisions for foundations on shrinkable clay and states that foundations need to reach a depth where anticipated ground movement will not affect the stability of the building, taking vegetation and trees into account. It gives indicative minimum depths for low, medium and high shrinkage clays, but these are not a substitute for assessing the actual site.

You can read the government's current collection of structural guidance through GOV.UK Approved Document A.

Do Trees Make Clay Foundations More Difficult?

Yes, trees can make a significant difference. This is something we always want to know about before digging foundations on clay ground.

Tree roots can take moisture from the surrounding soil. On shrinkable clay, that loss of moisture can contribute to the soil shrinking. The potential influence depends on factors such as the species and size of the tree, its distance from the proposed foundation and the characteristics of the soil.

This is why saying “the foundation only needs to be a metre deep” without looking at the site can be misleading. A nearby mature tree can change the foundation requirements considerably.

On jobs around Barking, we therefore look at the garden and surrounding property rather than just the footprint of the new foundation. Trees, hedges, previous structures, drains, changes in ground level and existing foundations can all tell you something about what is happening below ground.

What Happens When You Dig Into Clay?

Clay can look perfectly solid when it is exposed, but that does not automatically mean it is suitable at every depth. Excavation needs to continue until suitable founding material is reached and the proposed foundation requirements are satisfied.

One common mistake is leaving loose material, disturbed soil or old garden fill beneath a new foundation. You can have a beautifully level concrete pour on top, but if the material underneath is inconsistent, the finished structure is only as reliable as what it is sitting on.

We have also seen gardens where an old shed, patio, extension or outbuilding has previously occupied the area. Once removed, the ground may contain a mixture of old hardcore, made ground and natural clay. That needs to be identified during excavation rather than simply covered over.

Can You Pour Concrete Directly Onto Clay?

Not as a general rule. A foundation is not simply a matter of digging a hole and filling it with concrete.

The excavation needs to provide a suitable founding level and the concrete foundation needs to be appropriate for the structure being supported. Depending on the project, this could involve strip foundations, trench fill foundations or another engineered solution.

The correct approach depends on what is being built. A small garden structure does not impose the same loads as a house extension, garage or other structural building. The foundation therefore needs to be considered in relation to the actual project rather than using a standard depth or thickness for everything.

What About Wet Clay?

Wet clay needs particular care during excavation. Once clay has been disturbed and softened, you do not want to treat it as though it is exactly the same material as the firm clay originally encountered.

This is where practical site experience becomes important. If the bottom of an excavation has become soft, muddy or disturbed, the answer is not simply to carry on and pour concrete because the concrete lorry has arrived. The ground needs to be suitable for the foundation before the pour takes place.

Weather can also affect the programme. Heavy rain can turn an otherwise straightforward excavation into a job requiring additional preparation. Experienced groundwork contractors allow for this rather than trying to rush a foundation pour onto poor ground.

Does Clay Soil Mean Foundations Will Crack?

No. Clay soil does not automatically mean that a foundation will crack. Properly designed and constructed foundations can perform successfully on clay ground.

The problem usually comes when ground movement has not been properly considered. A shallow foundation in the wrong location, inadequate preparation, uncontrolled fill or nearby vegetation can all create problems that might have been avoided during the initial groundwork.

Another thing worth remembering is that not every crack in a building means the foundation has failed. Cracks can have many causes, so if an existing property is showing movement, the cause should be assessed rather than immediately assuming that the clay is responsible.

What We Look For Before Installing Foundations on Clay

Before starting a foundation project in Barking, there are several practical things we want to establish. The exact process depends on the project, but generally we look at:

  • The proposed size and purpose of the structure
  • Existing ground levels and changes in elevation
  • The type and condition of the material exposed during excavation
  • Nearby trees, hedges and significant vegetation
  • Previous buildings, patios, slabs or filled areas
  • Existing foundations and neighbouring structures
  • Drainage and other underground services
  • Site access and the practical requirements of excavation and concrete delivery

These details give a much better picture of what is actually happening on the site than simply looking at a soil type on a map.

So, Can You Build Foundations on Clay Soil in Barking?

Absolutely, but the clay needs to be treated as part of the foundation design rather than ignored. Good foundation work starts below ground, and the concrete itself is only one part of the finished system.

Around Barking, we commonly encounter gardens and residential plots where ground has been altered over the years. The natural clay may be mixed with previous construction material, levels may have changed and nearby trees can influence the soil. These are the sorts of details that need to be considered before deciding how a foundation should be built.

If you're planning an extension, garage, garden building or another structure and you're concerned about clay soil, it is worth dealing with the ground conditions properly at the start. Getting the foundation right is considerably easier than trying to correct movement after the structure has been built.
