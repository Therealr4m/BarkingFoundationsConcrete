Barking Concrete Floor Installation

Our concrete floor installation service in Barking covers new residential floors and concrete bases for homeowners preparing to install garden buildings, storage sheds and other small domestic structures.

A concrete floor needs to provide more than a finished surface. We prepare each installation around the ground underneath, the size of the building and the weight it will carry once installed.

Where garden ground is soft, uneven or has previously been disturbed, we remove unsuitable material and prepare a properly compacted sub-base before laying concrete. This helps reduce the risk of settlement, cracking and movement after the building is in place.

We also work around the access restrictions common to residential properties in Barking, including narrow side pathways and rear gardens where larger equipment cannot easily reach the installation area.

Residential Concrete Floor Installation For Garden Buildings

We install concrete floors for small garden sheds, timber outbuildings and residential storage structures requiring a permanent, stable base.

For a new garden shed, we establish the correct footprint, finished floor height and concrete specification before starting excavation. We also check how the shed will sit on the finished concrete, including its fixing points and any requirements supplied by the manufacturer.

Concrete Floors For New Garden Sheds

Our shed floor installations provide a firm supporting surface for timber, metal and prefabricated garden structures. We prepare the ground, install the supporting sub-base and finish the concrete to the levels required for the building.

We account for the shed's dimensions, expected storage loads and door clearance so the finished base works with the structure rather than creating problems during assembly.

Concrete Floors For Garden Storage Buildings

Garden storage buildings often carry concentrated loads from shelving, tools and heavy equipment. We establish the intended use before deciding on slab thickness and whether additional reinforcement is appropriate.

This ensures the floor is prepared for its actual working load instead of simply pouring concrete to a standard depth regardless of what will be placed on top.

Replacement Concrete Garden Floors

We also replace existing garden concrete floors where settlement, significant cracking or poor ground preparation has left the surface unsuitable for a new building.

Where an old slab has failed, we check the supporting ground before replacing it. Pouring fresh concrete over an unstable base can leave the same underlying problem unresolved.

Concrete Floor Ground Preparation On Soft Garden Soil

A large part of our residential concrete floor work involves preparing garden ground that is not immediately suitable for a concrete installation.

Soft soil, old planting areas, buried roots and previously disturbed ground can all affect how well a new slab is supported. We assess these conditions before excavation so the floor is not relying on loose or unsuitable material.

Excavation & Compacted Sub-Base Installation

We excavate to the required formation level, remove unsuitable material and install a suitable granular sub-base in compacted layers. Where ground conditions require additional treatment, we establish this before concrete work begins.

The sub-base provides a stable working platform and helps distribute loads across the prepared ground. We check levels and compaction before setting out the concrete slab.

Ground Moisture & Surface Drainage

We also consider surrounding garden levels and where rainwater will drain once the concrete floor is installed.

For enclosed sheds, the floor is normally finished level to suit the building, with surrounding ground and drainage arranged to direct water away. Where moisture protection is required, we allow for an appropriate damp-proof membrane and its connection to the building detail.

Concrete Floor Installation With Restricted Garden Access

Many concrete floor installations in Barking take place behind terraced and semi-detached properties where access is limited to narrow side passages or pathways between buildings.

We plan how excavated soil, sub-base materials and concrete will be moved before work starts. Where machinery cannot reach the garden, we organise smaller equipment and controlled material movement to complete the installation.

Concrete Supply & Pouring Arrangements

Depending on the slab size, access and required concrete specification, we arrange suitable concrete supply from a batching plant or use an appropriate on-site mixing method for smaller installations.

For restricted access, we assess whether wheelbarrow movement, a concrete pump or another practical delivery arrangement is suitable. The aim is to complete the pour consistently without unnecessary delays or disruption around the property.

We also plan the removal of excavated material and keep access routes organised throughout the work, particularly where the homeowner still needs to use the garden or side entrance.

Our Concrete Floor Installation Process In Barking

We follow a straightforward installation process, with the groundwork and concrete specification arranged around the building being installed.

1

Site Assessment & Excavation

We check the proposed floor dimensions, garden access, ground conditions and finished levels before excavating to the required depth.

2

Sub-Base & Floor Preparation

Concrete Pouring & Finishing

Concrete Floor Thickness, Reinforcement & Long-Term Performance

We establish the concrete floor specification around the intended structure, the loads it will carry and the condition of the supporting ground.

A small garden shed does not automatically need the same floor specification as a larger workshop or building carrying heavy equipment. We assess the actual requirements rather than treating every residential slab as the same installation.

Concrete Thickness & Reinforcement

We determine the appropriate slab thickness and concrete grade for the project. Where reinforcement is specified, we position suitable steel mesh or other reinforcement with the required support and concrete cover.

Reinforcement helps control cracking and supports the intended slab design, but it does not compensate for poorly prepared ground. We make sure the supporting layers are dealt with before concrete is placed.

Concrete Finishing & Curing

We finish the surface to suit the building and intended use, checking levels and edges before the concrete begins to set.

Once placed, we protect the concrete and allow for suitable curing. We advise when the surface can be accessed and when the shed or other structure can be installed, based on the concrete specification and expected loading.

Preventing Concrete Floor Movement & Structural Problems

One of the main reasons we put so much attention into ground preparation is that problems underneath a concrete floor often become more expensive to correct once a garden building has been installed.

Uneven Settlement & Building Movement

If a slab is installed over soft or poorly compacted ground, sections can settle unevenly. This may leave the shed out of level, affect door alignment and place unnecessary stress on the building frame.

We reduce this risk by removing unsuitable ground, preparing the supporting layers properly and checking the base before the concrete pour.

Cracking & Unsupported Slab Edges

Poorly supported edges, inconsistent sub-base compaction and unsuitable slab specifications can contribute to cracking and deterioration.

We pay particular attention to slab edges and the areas carrying the building's walls, making sure the floor is prepared for the intended load arrangement.

Water Collecting Around The Building

Incorrect finished levels can leave rainwater collecting against the shed or running towards its entrance.

We check surrounding ground levels and drainage arrangements before installation so the finished floor works with the garden layout and does not create avoidable water problems.
