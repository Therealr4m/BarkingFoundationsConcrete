Concrete Bases Prepared for Stratford Properties

A concrete base needs to suit both the structure it will support and the ground beneath it. We assess the intended use, the size of the installation and the existing ground before deciding how the area should be prepared.

Why the preparation matters

Problems often begin below the surface. Loose ground, an inconsistent sub-base or poorly compacted material can leave sections of concrete without adequate support. Over time, this may lead to cracking, uneven areas or movement beneath the building.

We do not treat excavation and sub-base preparation as minor steps before the pour. We remove unsuitable material where necessary, establish a suitable formation and compact the sub-base appropriately for the job. Reinforcement, concrete thickness and other construction details are considered according to the intended loading and site requirements.

The aim is to give the finished base the support it needs from the ground upwards, rather than relying on the concrete alone to compensate for poor preparation.

Working Around Terraced Housing and Narrow Garden Access in Stratford

On Stratford's more closely spaced residential streets, getting materials into a rear garden can be just as important as preparing the ground itself. Narrow side passages, boundary walls, existing paving and established landscaping can all restrict the route available to the working area.

Planning the work around your property

Before starting, we consider the width and condition of the access route, the distance from the delivery point to the proposed base and whether excavation equipment can reach the garden. Where machinery cannot pass through, the work may need a different approach to excavation and material handling.

Concrete delivery also needs to be planned around the site. Depending on the access, the volume required and the distance to the pour, we assess the practical delivery method before the installation date. This helps avoid unnecessary disruption and reduces the risk of delays once the concrete arrives.

We also take account of existing paths, fences and garden finishes that need to remain in place. If your property has restricted access, explain this when you enquire so we can factor it into the initial assessment.

Residential Concrete Base Work Across Stratford

We undertake concrete base work for homeowners making practical improvements to their properties. Some projects involve preparing a new area of garden, while others require an existing base to be removed or replaced because it has become uneven, cracked or unsuitable for the intended structure.

Every site is different. A relatively straightforward garden installation may only require local excavation and a prepared sub-base, while a larger or more heavily loaded structure can need additional groundwork and a different concrete specification.

Getting the base right for its intended use

We establish what the base needs to support before deciding on the installation approach. Ground condition, drainage, finished levels and the expected load all influence the preparation required.

Where an old concrete slab is being replaced, we look at the condition of the existing area and consider why it has failed. Simply pouring fresh concrete over a moving or poorly supported surface can leave the underlying problem unresolved. Addressing the ground properly gives the replacement a better starting point.

If you already have an existing slab, photographs of any cracking, sinking or uneven edges can help us understand the situation before arranging the next steps.

Ground Conditions and Concrete Base Stability in Stratford

Ground conditions can vary between properties, even along the same residential street. Clay-heavy soil is present in parts of East London and can change in volume as moisture levels rise and fall. The actual ground at your property needs to be assessed rather than assumed from the postcode alone.

Reducing the risk of movement and cracking

When the ground beneath a concrete base is poorly prepared, changes in moisture and inadequate support can contribute to settlement, cracking or uneven sections. Drainage, existing fill and previous garden alterations can also affect how the site needs to be prepared.

We account for the condition of the formation and the purpose of the base before settling on the appropriate construction details. Where the project requires a more specific foundation design, we establish the relevant requirements before proceeding.

Taking time over the work beneath the concrete is important. Once the base has been poured and a building installed on top, correcting underlying problems can be considerably more disruptive and expensive than preparing the ground properly at the outset.
